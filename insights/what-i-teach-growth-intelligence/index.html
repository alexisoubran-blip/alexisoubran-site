What I Teach About Growth Intelligence After Running Real Marketing Budgets

The recurring principles I teach across analytics, growth intelligence and executive marketing programs after applying them in operating environments.

September 22, 20267 minAlexis Soubran

Teaching forces a useful constraint: if a framework only works when the person presenting it is in the room, the framework is probably underdeveloped.

Across programs with EDEM/Merca2.0 and Colectivo23, I keep returning to a small set of principles because they survive across industries, platforms and budget sizes.

1. Start with the business model

Growth strategy is different for a high-margin subscription product, a dealership network, an ecommerce brand and a B2B sales organization. Before channel tactics, understand gross margin, sales cycle, repeat behavior and the company's cash constraints.

2. Metrics need a causal story

A dashboard is not analysis. If conversion fell, what changed in traffic mix, message, price, UX, inventory or sales response? The analyst's job is to generate and test explanations, not narrate the chart.

3. Marketing and sales are one data system

For lead businesses, campaign optimization that ends at the form is incomplete. Qualification, response time, meeting attendance and Win Rate belong in the growth loop.

4. Creative is measurable

Brand judgment and performance measurement are compatible. Hooks, demonstrations, objections, creators and formats can be tested without reducing creativity to a spreadsheet.

5. Experiment design matters more than experiment volume

Running many tests is easy. Running tests with a clear hypothesis, credible measurement and pre-agreed decision rule is harder and more valuable.

6. AI increases the value of judgment

As production and analysis become cheaper, choosing the right problem and interpreting evidence becomes more important. Senior marketing work shifts toward system design, prioritization and governance.

7. The weekly decision is the unit of progress

A good operating cadence produces a limited number of clear actions: move budget, change a message, fix a handoff, run a test, kill an activity. The report exists to support those decisions.

Teaching principleIf a student or operator cannot use the concept to make a different decision on Monday morning, the concept needs more work.

That is also why I continue teaching. Converting operating experience into something another person can apply is one of the best stress tests for whether the experience has actually become knowledge.
